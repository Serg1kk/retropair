# How the assessment works

sessionretro reads the logs of your sessions with a coding agent for a period and answers one
question: **how you lead the agent** — how you frame the task, verify the result and keep the
decisions in your hands. Code quality, architecture and speed are not assessed. This page explains
how a text comes out of the logs; the eight axes in detail are described in Russian in
[axes.md](../ru/axes.md).

## The eight axes

| Axis | What it looks at |
|---|---|
| Verifying results | whether you check what the agent did before accepting it; the most important axis, it counts twice in the level |
| Framing the task | what you put into the prompt before the agent starts, and when |
| Owning the decisions | whether you stay the author of the decisions once the agent writes the code |
| Changing course after failure | what you do when the result does not fit |
| Managing context | what fills the model's window, what goes into files, what you do when the window swells |
| Breaking down the work | how you slice the work and where each piece goes: the same chat, a new one, a subagent |
| Calibrating trust in the agent | whether trust changes with the cost of a mistake |
| Keeping changes reversible | how cheap it is to undo a bad move by the agent |

## Two stages

The assessment always runs in two stages, even when there are few logs for the period.

1. **Collectors.** An agent reads the logs and writes down verbatim quotes: where you set the
   frame, where you checked the result, where you took the agent's report on trust. It gives no
   scores at all and does not see the person card (role, grade, experience): knowing the grade, it
   is easy to start reading the log to fit it.
2. **Judge.** A second agent combines the facts of all collectors, scores each of the eight axes,
   checks last period's recommendations and writes the text for the person. It picks the wording
   from the person card: the same point sounds different to a beginner and to an architect with
   twenty years of experience. The score itself does not depend on the card.

Why not one agent: a score from reading a raw log directly and a score from combined facts are
obtained in different ways and compare poorly. The path is the same for everyone, so growth from
period to period shows up as growth, not as a difference in how it was counted.

## Batches: nothing is cut

If there are many sessions in a period, they are split into **batches** by start time: each
batch is read by its own collector, all in parallel. This is a split, not a sample: every usable
session of the period is assessed. A session is never split between batches. The batch size is
counted by your messages, not by file size, so long tool traces take no room.

Sessions with fewer than four of your messages (`batches.min_turns` in `config.json`) do not go
into batches: there is nothing to say about them. They do not disappear silently: they are marked
"too short" and are not fed again next time.

## A score only under a quote

Each axis gets a score from 0 to 4 **only under a verbatim quote** from the log with an address
(session and turn). No quote, no score. A script checks this after the judge and rejects a score
without a quote.

## Insufficient data is not a failure

A log does not show everything: you may have checked the result in another terminal, read the
diff yourself or discussed a decision by voice. So every axis has an honest answer,
**"insufficient data"**. It is given when the behaviour is not visible in the log and **never
turns into a low score**. A low score needs positive evidence: the log shows a result accepted
without a check.

## What is not penalised

- short messages and voice input;
- no tests on a project that has no tests at all;
- the number of iterations and attempts: iterating is normal work;
- irritation, caps lock and exclamation marks;
- where a prompt came from: copied, found or generated by a model;
- the quality of the project rules file: that would assess an artifact, not the logs.

## The level is computed by a formula

The overall level is set by a script by rule, not by a model by feel:

- the scored axes are taken (without "insufficient data"); verifying results counts twice;
- the **median** is computed: from 3.5 "Orchestrator", from 2.75 "Confident driver", from 2.0
  "Competent", from 1.0 "Naive", below that "Passenger";
- fewer than three scored axes: the level is "insufficient data";
- the gate sets a ceiling: one trigger (for example, a large change accepted blindly or a "done"
  report taken without any check) caps the level at "Competent", two or more at "Developing". The
  gate only reacts to what is in the log; silence never triggers it.

The judge names its own level too, but only as a cross-check: the reviewer sees any mismatch.

## Recommendations and the next period

The text has one to three "try differently" items: the place in the log with its date and quote,
what it cost and a concrete action. Each action is a recommendation with its own id in the run
tracker. Next period the judge looks for it in the new logs: "followed", "partly", "not followed"
or "not visible in the logs". "Not visible" does not mean "ignored": the recommendation stays open.
"Partly" also keeps it open; only "followed" and "not followed" close it.

A recommendation is judged only on logs recorded after the person received it (in self mode that
is the run time; in team and course with reviewer approval, the approval time). If every log of
the period ended earlier, for example when a backlog of history is reviewed period after period,
the only verdict is "too early to judge": the recommendation stays open for the next period.

## What the person sees

The text: an opening, the "try differently" items and a walk through all eight axes by their
human names. There are no scores or level in the text; in self mode you can turn them on for
yourself with `show_scores` in `config.json`. There are never comparisons with other people, in
any mode. In team and course modes a reviewer reads the text first. Logs are voluntary: no logs,
no text and no conclusions about the person.

## How well it works

The judge is checked against a golden set of 15 real sessions with reference scores from earlier
approved reviews, in two full runs (opus judge, sonnet collectors): 96–100 % of axis scores are
within ±1 of the reference, the runs differ by at most one point on any axis, and the overall
level matched in 9 of 15 sessions. ±1 is a weak bar when most scores sit between 2 and 4, and the
judge tends to pull scores towards the middle, so read the quotes and the next steps rather than
the numbers. The axis references are in Russian; the judge's English text has not been checked on
the golden set yet.
